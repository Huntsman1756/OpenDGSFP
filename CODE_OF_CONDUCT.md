# Code of conduct

OpenDGSFP aims to keep technical discussion rigorous, evidence-based and respectful.

## Expected behavior

Contributors should:

- critique claims, code and evidence rather than people;
- distinguish observed facts from inference;
- correct mistakes openly when evidence changes;
- provide reproducible context for technical or data-quality claims;
- respect source licensing, attribution and privacy constraints;
- keep issue and pull-request discussion focused on the project;
- assume disagreement is part of review, not a personal conflict.

## Unacceptable behavior

The project does not accept:

- harassment, threats or discriminatory abuse;
- personal attacks or doxxing;
- publishing private credentials, personal data or exploit details without a legitimate need and appropriate channel;
- deliberately misleading evidence or fabricated test results;
- repeated disruption of issues or reviews after maintainers have set scope;
- license or attribution removal from third-party work.

## Technical disputes

Where contributors disagree, the preferred resolution order is:

1. primary source or specification;
2. reproducible fixture/test;
3. explicit documented assumption;
4. maintainer decision when evidence does not uniquely determine the design.

A maintainer decision can set project scope, but it does not convert an uncertain factual claim into a verified fact; uncertainty should remain documented.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide or remove abusive/off-topic content and may restrict participation for repeated violations. Good-faith technical criticism, including strong disagreement with a design choice, is not a conduct violation when expressed professionally.

Security-sensitive material should follow `SECURITY.md` rather than being posted publicly.
